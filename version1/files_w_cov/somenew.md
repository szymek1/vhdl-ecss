# Modeling a Two-Phase Hybrid Stepper Motor for Closed-Loop Validation of the MCE-NG-IP Controller

## TL;DR
- Your plant's backward drift is a **phase-sequence/convention mismatch compounded by loss of synchronism**: the current vector rotates opposite to the sense the plant's torque law assumes, and the hard 0→400 steps/s start (no ramp) with essentially zero damping (ζ≈0.005) means the rotor never locks to the field — so ωm is merely buffeted at the electrical frequency with a slightly negative mean instead of settling at +12.57 rad/s, and θm walks to ≈ −0.23 rad while the controller counts +7.3 steps.
- The slide model has concrete, fixable errors: back-EMF and torque must use the **electrical angle θe = Np·θm (not mechanical θm)**; the phase-B back-EMF must be a **cos term of opposite sign to A's sin** (the slide writes cos for both); `arctan(ia/ib)` must be **atan2(ib, ia)**; the detent term must be **−Kd·sin(4·Np·θm)** (4th harmonic of the electrical angle, 200 cycles/rev), not harmonics of the mechanical angle; and **all dissipation (viscous B·ω, Coulomb Tc, load TL) is missing entirely.**
- The canonical, citable plant is the Bodson–Chiasson / Simscape model: `L·dia/dt = ua − R·ia + Kt·ωm·sin(Npθm)`, `L·dib/dt = ub − R·ib − Kt·ωm·cos(Npθm)`, `Te = Kt(−ia·sin(Npθm) + ib·cos(Npθm)) − Kd·sin(4Npθm)`, `J·dωm/dt = Te − B·ωm − Tc·tanh(ωm/ωs) − TL`, with the mandatory identity **Kt = Np·Ke**. Fully-parameterized Version 1 (ideal) and Version 2 (with disturbances), a parameter table with provenance, a fix-verification checklist, and corrected C++ are given below.

## Key Findings

1. **A hybrid stepper is a two-phase PM synchronous machine.** With Np = 50 rotor tooth-pairs (1.8°/step → 200 steps/rev), the permanent-magnet flux linked by each phase is λa = Ke·cos(Np·θm), λb = Ke·sin(Np·θm). Every voltage, back-EMF and torque term follows from these two flux linkages by Faraday's law and co-energy. This is precisely the model documented by MathWorks Simscape, defined by Bodson, Chiasson, Novotnak & Rekowski ("High-Performance Nonlinear Feedback Control of a Permanent Magnet Stepper Motor," *IEEE Transactions on Control Systems Technology*, Vol. 1, No. 1, pp. 5–14, March 1993), [Utah](https://my.ece.utah.edu/~bodson/publications.html) and used in the recent Eindhoven/Canon/ASML work (Fan, Bolderman, Koekebakker, Butler & Lazar, "Physics-guided neural networks for inversion-based feedforward control applied to hybrid stepper motors," arXiv:2306.12817, 2023). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.12817)

2. **The identity Kt = Np·Ke is mandatory and unit-consistent.** Torque is Te = Np·Ke·(−ia·sin θe + ib·cos θe) = Kt·im·sin(δ), where δ = θe_cmd − Np·θm is the load angle. For your motor Ke ≈ 3.8 mV·s/rad × Np = 50 gives Kt = 0.19 N·m/A — which is exactly the MathWorks Simscape Stepper Motor block's documented default "Motor torque constant — 0.19 N*m/A." That is consistent with the ~0.40 N·m two-phase-on holding torque at im = √2·1.5 ≈ 2.12 A. Setting Km and Ke independently injects a spurious energy source or sink.

3. **Detent torque is a 4th harmonic of the electrical angle**, Td = −Kd·sin(4·Np·θm): one cycle per full step, 200 cycles per revolution for a 200-step motor. Portescap states it plainly — detent "is a 4th harmonic of the torque generated by one of the motor phases"; [Portescap](https://www.portescap.com/en/newsroom/whitepapers/2023/08/a-guide-to-stepper-motor-terminology-and-parameters) the MDPI micro-claw-pole study confirms "the period of the detent torque is a 90° electrical angle." [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-666X/13/6/931) MathWorks writes the term verbatim as "Te = −Km(iA−eA/Rm)sin(Nrθ) + Km(iB−eB/Rm)cos(Nrθ) − Td·sin(4Nrθ)," [mathworks](https://www.mathworks.com/help/sps/ref/steppermotor.html) with "Td is the detent torque amplitude." [mathworks](https://www.mathworks.com/help/sps/ref/steppermotor.html) Your slide's κ0·sin(θm)+κ1·sin(2θm)+κr·sin(4θm) are harmonics of the *mechanical* revolution — those model eccentricity/imbalance/misalignment (1× and 2× per rev), **not** tooth-scale detent.

4. **The slide's mechanical model has no dissipation whatsoever.** With no B·ω, no Coulomb, no load, the rotor-field system is a nearly loss-free oscillator at ωn ≈ 1832 rad/s. Forward-Euler integration of an almost-undamped resonance *adds* energy, so any transient rings for the whole 18 ms (or grows). This alone can produce a permanent oscillation independent of the direction bug.

5. **The observed ±25…−50 rad/s oscillation at the electrical frequency with a negative mean is loss of synchronism from a slipping, backward-rotating field** — not benign "hunting." Two compounding causes: (i) the current vector rotates the wrong way relative to the plant's sin/cos assignment, so mean torque is negative; (ii) a 0→400 steps/s step demand pushes the load angle far past ±90° (the pull-out limit), so the rotor never locks and is instead shaken at the slip (≈ electrical) frequency.

## Details

### A. How the equations are built from physics (pedagogical tutorial)

**Step 1 — Flux linkage (the one modeling assumption).** A hybrid stepper's magnetized, toothed rotor presents Np = 50 magnetic cycles per mechanical revolution. Assuming a sinusoidal air-gap field (i.e. ignoring the permeance space harmonics — the explicit standard assumption in the two-phase HSM Simulink literature), [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/98318406/Simple_Mathematical_and_Simulink_Model_of_Stepper_Motor) the PM flux linked by the two orthogonal phase windings is:

  λa(θm) = Ke·cos(Np·θm)   λb(θm) = Ke·sin(Np·θm)

Ke is the peak flux linkage per phase (units V·s = Wb-turns). Defining the **electrical angle** θe ≡ Np·θm is the single most important bookkeeping step: the electrical world turns Np = 50 times faster than the shaft. At 400 full steps/s the shaft turns at 12.57 rad/s (2 rev/s) while θe advances at 628 rad/s (100 Hz electrical).

**Step 2 — Voltage equation (Faraday + Ohm).** Each phase is a series R–L plus the motional EMF dλ/dt:

  ua = R·ia + L·dia/dt + dλa/dt,  dλa/dt = −Ke·Np·ωm·sin(Np·θm)
  ub = R·ib + L·dib/dt + dλb/dt,  dλb/dt = +Ke·Np·ωm·cos(Np·θm)

so the back-EMFs are ea = −Ke·Np·ωm·sin θe and eb = +Ke·Np·ωm·cos θe. Because **Ke·Np = Kt**, the back-EMF coefficient equals Kt. This is verbatim the MathWorks Simscape form "eA = −Km·ω·sin(Nrθ)" and "eB = Km·ω·cos(Nrθ)," [mathworks](https://www.mathworks.com/help/sps/ref/steppermotor.html) and the Eindhoven HSM form (arXiv:2306.12817, Eq. 3): "L·di_a/dt = v_a − R·i_a + k_m(dy/dt)sin(Ny)," "L·di_b/dt = v_b − R·i_b − k_m(dy/dt)cos(Ny)." [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.12817v1) [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.12817) Solved for the current states you integrate:

  dia/dt = (ua − R·ia + Kt·ωm·sin θe) / L
  dib/dt = (ub − R·ib − Kt·ωm·cos θe) / L

**Step 3 — Torque (co-energy).** For a magnetically linear machine, Te = ∂W′co/∂θm at constant current = ia·dλa/dθm + ib·dλb/dθm:

  Te = ia·(−Ke·Np·sin θe) + ib·(+Ke·Np·cos θe) = Kt·(−ia·sin θe + ib·cos θe)

For a balanced commanded current vector ia = im·cos(θe_cmd), ib = im·sin(θe_cmd):

  Te = Kt·im·sin(θe_cmd − θe) = Kt·im·sin(δ)

This is the fundamental static torque–load-angle law (Acarnley, *Stepping Motors: A Guide to Theory and Practice*, 4th ed., IET 2002; Kenjo & Sugawara, *Stepping Motors and Their Microprocessor Controls*, 2nd ed., Oxford 1994): [Utah](https://my.ece.utah.edu/~bodson/pdf/Spontaneous%20Speed%20Reversals%20in%20Stepper%20Motors.pdf) static torque is a sinusoid of the load angle δ with peak equal to the holding torque = Kt·im. The peak occurs at δ = 90°; beyond that the machine loses synchronism (pull-out). Jones' "Stepping Motor Physics" (University of Iowa) notes the torque-vs-position curve of a two-winding hybrid has period 4S (four steps) [Matt Bognar](https://homepage.divms.uiowa.edu/~jones/step/physics.html) — the same 4th-harmonic structure that governs detent.

**Step 4 — Newton.** J·dωm/dt = Te + Tdetent − B·ωm − Tc·sgn(ωm) − TL; dθm/dt = ωm.

**Step 5 — dq view and load angle.** The Park transform into the rotor frame yields id (flux-producing, does no work) and iq (torque-producing): Te = Kt·iq. Field-oriented control holds δ = 90° (id = 0) so torque-per-amp is maximized — the "load-angle control" of the FPGA HSM literature (ResearchGate 328151614). For your open-loop velocity plant, δ is set by the load exactly as in a PM synchronous machine; logging δ = θe_cmd − Np·θm is the single most informative diagnostic signal.

**Rotor hunting dynamics (the resonance behind your problem).** Linearizing J·d²θ/dt² = −B·dθ/dt + Kt·im·sin(θe − Np·θm) about a detent equilibrium (Bodson, Sato & Silver, "Spontaneous Speed Reversals in Stepper Motors," Univ. of Utah) [utah](https://my.ece.utah.edu/~bodson/pdf/Spontaneous%20Speed%20Reversals%20in%20Stepper%20Motors.pdf) gives the characteristic polynomial:

  s² + (B/J)·s + (Kt·im·Np)/J = 0 [utah](https://my.ece.utah.edu/~bodson/pdf/Spontaneous%20Speed%20Reversals%20in%20Stepper%20Motors.pdf)

so the undamped natural frequency and damping ratio are:

  **ωn = √(Kt·im·Np / J)**  and  **ζ = B / (2·√(J·Kt·im·Np))**

For your parameters ωn = √(0.19·2.12·50 / 6e-6) ≈ **1832 rad/s** (≈ 292 Hz), and at B = 1e-4, ζ = 1e-4 / (2·√(6e-6·0.19·2.12·50)) ≈ **0.005** — essentially undamped. This is why any transient rings for the entire 18 ms window. Industry sources give the same spring-mass form (fn = (1/2π)√(N·T_holding / J)); [Automation.com](https://www.automation.com/en-us/articles/november-2021/strategies-avoid-stepper-motor-resonance-problems) the magnetic field acts as a nonlinear torsional spring with stiffness Np·Kt·im.

### B. Equation-by-equation critique of the slide model

**Electrical model.**
- `Ua = R·ia + L·dia/dt + Ke·ωm·cos(Θm)` — **two errors.** (1) The angle must be electrical: cos(Np·Θm), not cos(Θm). Dropping Np makes the back-EMF advance 50× too slowly and be ~50× too small (the true coefficient is Ke·Np·ωm = Kt·ωm, not Ke·ωm). (2) With λa = Ke·cos θe, phase A's back-EMF is a **−sin** term (−Kt·ωm·sin θe); a bare +cos is the wrong trig function and wrong sign.
- `Ub = R·ib + L·dib/dt − Ke·ωm·cos(Θm)` — **phase B is written as cos when it must be cos of opposite sign to A's sin**, i.e. the correct pair is ea = −Kt·ωm·sin θe, eb = +Kt·ωm·cos θe. Writing A and B with the same cos function makes the two back-EMFs collinear, which is impossible for a quadrature machine and destroys the rotating field.
- **Your C++ EMF is actually correct where the slide is wrong**: `ia += dt/L·(volts(a) − R·ia + Ke·om·Np·sin(the))`, `ib += … − Ke·om·Np·cos(the)`, with `the = Np·θm` — it has the Np factor, the sin/cos split, and the right signs. Document this discrepancy and make the thesis text match the code (the code is the reference).

**Mechanical model.**
- `Θe = arctan(ia/ib)` — **three problems.** (1) Use `atan2`, not `arctan`, to preserve the quadrant over the full 0–2π electrical cycle; `arctan` folds everything into ±90° and produces discontinuities twice per cycle. (2) Argument order: with ia = im·cos θe and ib = im·sin θe, tan θe = ib/ia, so θe = atan2(ib, ia) — the slide's ia/ib is **inverted** (it computes 90°−θe). (3) You do not need to recover θe from the currents at all for torque; the co-energy form uses the **rotor** electrical angle Np·θm directly and is far more robust. Recover θe_cmd = atan2(ib, ia) only for logging/diagnostics.
- `im = sqrt(ia²+ib²)` — **correct** (current-vector magnitude).
- `M_drive = Km·im·sin(Θe − Θm)` — **wrong angle.** The load angle is electrical: δ = Θe − **Np**·Θm. Using Θm makes the magnetic spring 50× too soft, mislocating ωn by ~7×. Clarify that Km = Kt = Np·Ke. Your code's `Te = Kt·(−ia·sin(the)+ib·cos(the))` with `the = Np·θm` is the correct, preferred implementation — again the code beats the slide.
- `M_dist = κ0·sin(Θm+φ0) + κ1·sin(2Θm+φ1) + κr·sin(4Θm+φr)` — **mislabeled as detent.** These are 1×, 2×, 4× per *mechanical revolution*. Detent is 4·Np = 200× per revolution: Td = −Kd·sin(4·Np·Θm). The κ terms are legitimate but represent **rotor eccentricity / magnet imbalance (1× rev) and stator ovality or coupling misalignment (2× rev)** — per-revolution disturbances. Keep them as an *optional*, correctly-labeled eccentricity block in Version 2.
- `M_mismatch = ½·im·(εa+εb)·sin(Θe+Θm)` — **right intent, wrong specifics.** Phase-to-phase asymmetry (unequal Kt per phase, ADC/current-sense gain mismatch) produces torque ripple at *twice* the electrical frequency and is driven by the *difference* (εa−εb), not the sum. A defensible form: Tmm = ½·Kt·im·(εa−εb)·sin(2·Np·θm), with εa, εb the dimensionless per-phase fractional gain errors (e.g. ±0.02). Off by default.
- **No B·ω, no Tc, no TL anywhere** — the largest single omission. Physically every real motor has viscous and Coulomb friction; numerically, forward Euler on the undamped 1832 rad/s resonance is unstable. Note that a **chopper/current-controlled drive removes the natural electrical damping** that a voltage drive provides through R·i and back-EMF feedback (Portescap: "Chopper drivers typically do not allow this type of damping since the current is kept constant despite back-EMF variations") [Automation.com](https://www.automation.com/en-us/articles/november-2021/strategies-avoid-stepper-motor-resonance-problems) — so in a current-controlled plant B must carry essentially all the damping, making its inclusion doubly important.

### C. VERSION 1 — Ideal motor (no disturbance torques)

Use this to prove the current loop, the phase mapping, and basic velocity tracking. "Ideal" means **no detent, no Coulomb, no eccentricity, no phase mismatch, no external load** — but keep a small viscous B, because a real motor always has some damping and because B = 0 makes forward Euler unstable at the resonance. State x = [ia, ib, ωm, θm].

  θe  = Np·θm
  dia/dt = (ua − R·ia + Kt·ωm·sin θe) / L
  dib/dt = (ub − R·ib − Kt·ωm·cos θe) / L
  Te     = Kt·(−ia·sin θe + ib·cos θe)
  dωm/dt = (Te − B·ωm) / J
  dθm/dt = ωm

If you insist on B = 0 for a "frictionless" reference, switch to semi-implicit (symplectic) Euler or RK4 and shrink dt; otherwise keep B ≈ 1e-4 N·m·s/rad as minimal physically-justified damping. For scale, the MathWorks Simscape Stepper Motor block's documented default rotor damping is 8.0e-4 N·m/(rad/s) and default rotor inertia 4.5e-5 kg·m².

Corrected C++ `step()` (Version 1, averaged-PWM plant):
```cpp
// state: ia, ib, wm, thm ; params: R, L, Kt, Np, J, B, dt
void step_ideal(double ua, double ub) {
    double the = Np * thm;                 // electrical angle
    double s = sin(the), c = cos(the);
    double dia = (ua - R*ia + Kt*wm*s) / L; // back-EMF +sin on A
    double dib = (ub - R*ib - Kt*wm*c) / L; // back-EMF -cos on B
    double Te  = Kt * (-ia*s + ib*c);       // co-energy torque
    double dwm = (Te - B*wm) / J;
    ia += dt*dia;  ib += dt*dib;            // forward Euler
    wm += dt*dwm;  thm += dt*wm;
}
```

### D. VERSION 2 — Realistic motor (with disturbances)

  θe   = Np·θm
  dia/dt = (ua − R·ia + Kt·ωm·sin θe) / L
  dib/dt = (ub − R·ib − Kt·ωm·cos θe) / L
  Te    = Kt·(−ia·sin θe + ib·cos θe)
  Tdet  = −Kd·sin(4·Np·θm)                        // detent, 4th electrical harmonic (200/rev)
  Tecc  = κ1·sin(θm+φ1) + κ2·sin(2θm+φ2)           // OPTIONAL eccentricity/imbalance (1×,2× per rev)
  Tmm   = ½·Kt·im·(εa−εb)·sin(2·Np·θm)             // OPTIONAL phase-mismatch ripple (2× electrical)
  Tfric = B·ωm + Tc·tanh(ωm/ωs)                    // viscous + smoothed Coulomb
  dωm/dt = (Te + Tdet + Tecc + Tmm − Tfric − TL) / J
  dθm/dt = ωm

The Coulomb term uses tanh(ωm/ωs) smoothing (ωs a small blending speed, e.g. 0.1–1 rad/s) to avoid the sgn() discontinuity that stalls fixed-step integrators; for true stiction near zero speed use the **Karnopp dead-band** model (treat |ωm| < ε as "stuck" and let static friction absorb up to a breakaway torque Ts before motion begins). Both are standard — see the review by Marques et al., "Review and comparison of dry friction force models," *Nonlinear Dynamics* (Springer, 2015). The Stribeck effect (friction dipping just above breakaway) can be added as Tc + (Ts−Tc)·exp(−(ωm/ωStr)²)·sgn(ωm) but is usually second-order for this application.

Corrected C++ `step()` (Version 2):
```cpp
void step_real(double ua, double ub) {
    double the = Np * thm;
    double s = sin(the), c = cos(the);
    double im = sqrt(ia*ia + ib*ib);
    double dia = (ua - R*ia + Kt*wm*s) / L;
    double dib = (ub - R*ib - Kt*wm*c) / L;
    double Te  = Kt * (-ia*s + ib*c);
    double Tdet = -Kd * sin(4.0*Np*thm);
    double Tmm  = 0.5*Kt*im*(eps_a - eps_b)*sin(2.0*Np*thm); // optional
    double Tfric = B*wm + Tc*tanh(wm/ws);
    double dwm = (Te + Tdet + Tmm - Tfric - TL) / J;
    ia += dt*dia;  ib += dt*dib;
    wm += dt*dwm;  thm += dt*wm;
}
```

In **switched H-bridge mode**, replace ua, ub by the instantaneous ±Vsupply set by the PWM comparator each sub-step; dt must then resolve the PWM period (several samples per PWM cycle). In **averaged mode**, ua = U0·(2d/range − 1) as you already have; averaged mode is sufficient whenever you are validating the current-control and mechanical behavior rather than switching ripple.

### F. Diagnosis of the failing 400-steps/s run
